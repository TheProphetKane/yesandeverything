The short answer

The cheapest house in the search by about $100,000, and the one that needs the most. It is a 1972 split-level that has barely been touched: original carpet, a 1970s kitchen, popcorn ceilings, a bare basement ceiling and a bathroom gutted to the studs. Once a full renovation is added, it lands near the price of the move-in-ready houses, and it is the smallest of them. It only makes sense if you want to do the work and pick every finish.

Problems and risks

Full interior renovation

Worn carpet, popcorn ceilings, a 1970s kitchen with vinyl floor and an almond refrigerator (photos 3 to 6), dated bedrooms and stairs.

Cost: Estimated $50,000 to $90,000 for kitchen, flooring, paint, ceilings and both baths, from typical St. Louis-area contractor ranges.

Gutted lower bathroom

Studs, old pipes and no fixtures (photo 20). It needs plumbing, a shower or tub, tile and a vanity.

Cost: Estimated $10,000 to $18,000.

Unknown furnace, air conditioner and water heater

The utility area shows an older water heater and exposed ductwork (photos 18, 19 and 21). Ask for ages.

Cost: Up to $9,500 to $16,500 if all three are original or near the end of their life (estimate).

Popcorn ceilings on a 1972 house

Test for asbestos before removal.

Cost: About $50 to $100 a sample; removal $1 to $7 a square foot depending on the test (estimate).

Big tree close to the house

A mature tree stands near the front corner (photo 1). Scope the sewer line for roots.

Cost: Sewer scope about $150 to $300.

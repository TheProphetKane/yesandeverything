The short answer

The cheapest house that passes the main-floor test: a 1958 brick ranch in old Wentzville near the interstate, with vinyl plank floors, granite and stainless already done and no association. Seventy-eight days on the market at $250,000 with only a $2,000 cut, in a market where the other houses here move in weeks, and the photos show why: the basement drywall is cut out along the bottom of every wall, the cleanup after water (photos 22 to 26), while the rest of the house is empty and virtually staged. On a 1958 house the questions are the sewer line, the electrical panel, the windows and the mechanicals, and here, first of all, where the water came from and whether the cause was fixed.

Problems and risks

1958 systems

Galvanized or cast-iron pipes, an old panel, original windows and a clay sewer line are all common in a house this age and none is mentioned as replaced.

Cost: Sewer scope $150 to $300; a panel $2,000 to $4,000; windows $8,000 to $15,000; all estimates.

Mechanicals of unknown age

No ages given.

Cost: Estimated $9,500 to $16,500 if the furnace, air conditioner and water heater are all old.

Water in the basement

The basement drywall has been cut out about two feet up in every room and the floor coverings removed (photos 22 to 26), the standard cleanup after a flood or a sewer backup. The questions are the cause, whether it was fixed, and whether there was mold. On a 1958 house a clay sewer line backing up is a common cause.

Cost: Estimated $15,000 to $30,000 to rebuild 1,000 square feet of basement finish, plus $3,000 to $8,000 for waterproofing or a backwater valve if the water came from outside or the sewer, typical St. Louis-area ranges.

Ask before making an offer

  1. What flooded the basement, when, and what was done about the cause? Ask for the seller's disclosure and any cleanup or mold report.
  2. Will the seller rebuild the basement finish, or credit it at closing?
  3. Ages of the roof, furnace, air conditioner, water heater and electrical panel.
  4. Has the sewer line ever been scoped or replaced?

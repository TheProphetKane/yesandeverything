The house plan · The basement layout

Main and upper floors, 1 square to the foot

The main and upper floors at 45 Autumn Crest Ct

These two plans are Burkemper Homes' own Rockport drawing, taken from its 2007 brochure and laid over the county's measured outline one square to the foot, sharing the basement layout's grid, so a spot on one floor sits right over the same spot on the floor below. Each room is colored by its use. Where the brochure prints a room's size, the plan carries it; every other size is read off the drawing and runs close, not exact. It is a sales drawing. An owner may have moved a wall since 2003, so the tape at the inspection settles anything that matters.

The furniture is a first placement, there to be moved. Pieces the family owns are drawn at the size Nick gave, and the rest at a common size; the table under each plan says which is which.

Carpet goes down on this whole floor ten to fourteen days after closing: builder grade, on a moisture-barrier pad, the main stair included. Nothing moves in until it is down. If the black light finds no urine in the pad, a rented cleaner stands in and the old carpet stays. Each bedroom gets a new smoke alarm the day after move-in. The hall's alarm also senses carbon monoxide.

Who sleeps where

The infant takes bedroom 4, at the rear in the middle. Its door is about 19 feet from the primary bedroom's, three feet farther than bedroom 2, but the room sits on the quiet back side of the house, away from the street noise and the headlights, which matters for daytime naps. One of its walls backs onto the primary toilet room, so the crib goes on the opposite wall.

The toddler takes bedroom 3, the rear corner next door. Both kids share the quiet side. Its door sits a few steps from the hall bath's, which helps in the potty-training years.

Guests get bedroom 2. At 12 by 11 feet it is the largest of the three, so the full bed has room for an end table on each side. It sits at the front, across the hall from both kids' doors, beside the hall bath.

The frogs go in the dining room. The shelf stands on an inside wall there, out of the sun from the front windows, and on the main floor any night calling stays away from the bedrooms. The loft spot stays drawn in dashes in case the dining room needs the wall.

Where the drawing may not match

  • The rear bays. The builder centers one on the breakfast room and one on the family room, and the plan follows that. The county's sketch puts the pair about four feet toward the garage.
  • The fridge. A dashed outline marks its spot on the drawing, where the counter ends beside the laundry door. The plan reads it that way.
  • The closets and the linen shelves. They are drawn as the brochure shows them. A previous owner may have changed any of them.

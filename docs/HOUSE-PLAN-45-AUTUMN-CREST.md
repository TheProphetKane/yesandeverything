# The house plan

Under contract since Sunday, November 1, 2026, at $330,000. Closing is by Monday, November 30, and the family moves in on Saturday, December 19, after a week away for Thanksgiving. This page lists every job planned for the house, in the order it happens, with two prices for each: what the materials cost if the family does the work, and what a contractor would charge.

## How to read the plan

- **Needed** means safety, code, damage, or something the house cannot be lived in without. **Wanted** means an upgrade, cosmetic work or a basement room, done when the money allows. **When it fails** marks a replacement that waits for the old one to quit, so it sits outside every total.
- **Family** prices the materials, because the family supplies the labor: Nick, Lexi, Nick's dad Bill, his brothers Gannan and AJ, Obo, who is Grandma, plus their friend Dom. Where a contractor does the job either way, the family price is the contractor's. **Hired** prices the same job done by a contractor at Saint Louis rates, labor included.
- Every price is an estimate. Each one was rechecked on November 3, 2026, every section ends on its own totals, and [everything on one list](#everything-on-one-list) at the bottom gathers every job by a short name, listing both of its prices without the dates.

November is paperwork and the inspection. The first two weeks of December go to the empty house: old carpet out, paint on every wall, new floors through the whole main level, new baseboard in the two rooms where it was pulled, new carpet upstairs, a deep clean. Move-in is December 19. The basement rooms and the deck fill January to May, with the bigger upgrades after that.

## What the sellers do, what the family does, and the conclusion

The sellers' two requirements, or there is no sale: a new roof and new siding before closing, both with transferable warranties. Both are hail-struck, and the hail is the requirement: the roof gets replaced whether it went on in 2003 or, as the sellers say, in 2018. The inspector calls the roof original, found hail dents on the vents, the siding and the screens, and holes in the siding on every side. At Saint Louis rates that is $25,000 to $43,000 of work: 25 to 27 squares of roof at $11,500 to $20,000, plus 30 to 33 of siding at $13,500 to $23,000, from ten local and national pages. None of it is in the family's totals.

The sellers' repairs and credit, asked once in writing with the six inspection reports attached, come to $15,900 to $29,900:

- the furnace that failed its carbon monoxide check on November 4, replaced on the existing coil, $3,500 to $5,500
- the radon system with its retest, $1,350 to $2,350
- the sump pump, with the dead outlet beside the pit, $775 to $1,600
- the water heater, $1,500 to $2,300
- gas line hangers, $150 to $300
- the deck, $2,800 to $4,000
- the chimney, at whatever scope a second sweep's Level 2 camera inspection finds, $2,500 to $7,000
- the glass in the sliders plus six windows, $2,300 to $4,600
- screens, the range, the kitchen drain, $825 to $1,850
- one handyman visit, $200 to $350

Every figure comes from at least three named pages, listed in the closing table below, behind each row's More button. A lender caps a seller's contribution, 3 to 6 percent of the price, $9,900 to $19,800 here, so the furnace, the radon system and the sump pump are asked as repairs done before closing and the rest as money.

The family's side is the budget at a glance above: every needed job, every wanted one, the two together, with the hired price beside each. Before move-in the floors with the carpet are the big line, $8,000 to $13,000 laid by the family with the store's installers; the alarms, the downspouts, the driveway come under $1,300 together.

The conclusion: buy, on the two requirements and the one list. A new roof and new siding on a $330,000 house leave it $62,000 under the twin next door before the family lifts a tool, and the list is what the reports wrote, priced from the pages.

## Where the plan saves

- **Windows:** the inspector's write-up of the rot goes on the credit request as repair value, and the family does the replacing its own way.
- **Roof and siding:** the sellers replace both before closing, so neither is in any family total; at Saint Louis rates that is $25,500 to $47,000 the family does not spend.
- **Upstairs carpet:** builder grade with a store's free installation instead of pet-rated nylon saves $1,100 to $3,000.
- **D&D room:** one layer of drywall instead of two saves $400 to $600, since the mineral wool does most of the work.
- **Play room, lounge and hall:** sealed concrete under rugs, with an open ceiling, saves about $1,200 to $1,600 over plank and drywall.
- **Deck:** the 12 by 16 treated deck the family builds, with the sellers' credit covering its materials if they agree to one.
- **Dumping:** the old carpet, the old deck and the job scraps go in the dumpster at the family's restaurant, so no family price carries a dump run.
- **Waiting:** the foyer and laundry tile, the plank outside the kitchen, the upstairs bath floors, the basement stair carpet and the door frames are all Wanted, so any of them can slip if money runs short.
- **Taxes and insurance:** the appeal is worth about $520 a year. Three insurance quotes keep the policy honest.

# The basement layouts for 45 Autumn Crest Ct

The basement is a 36 by 33 foot rectangle outside its 9-inch poured walls, about 34 by 31 inside, plus a 12 by 2 bump-out under the front porch. Two layouts are drawn here, both from Nick's marked-up shell of November 5 at 20:05 and the walk-through video of that afternoon. They share everything the house fixes: the stair, the bath rough-in, the walkout slider, the windows, the sump pit hard in the foundation corner, the furnace and the water heater 12 to 15 feet off the garage wall, two staggered posts under the far beam, the two electrical panels, and the sellers' finished room, kept as it stands. They also share the office, the lounge, the bath, the utility room, the hall and the 32-foot putting lane.

The one choice between them is which corner takes the D&D studio. The play room never sits beside the furnace, so the rear corner on the garage side holds either the studio or the bins; the finished one at the stair's foot takes whatever is left over. Every door in both drawings swings into open floor.

Every spot marked "about" comes from the marks, the photos and the video, not a tape. The basement is full of the sellers' belongings, so the chosen layout gets taped onto the slab once it is cleared, and a wall moves if the tape says so.

## What the walk-through and Nick's marks settled, in both layouts

- The finished room. It is about 11 feet wide along the front wall and 15 deep, with its doorway facing the stair's foot. A reach-in closet runs immediately inside on the right along the stair's framed side, its sliding doors gone and its rod popped loose. Where the closet ends the wall steps in further, and the room's north end is about 8 feet wide beyond that step, with room past the bed. The counter and the desk stand along the front wall. The panel sits in its rough cutout on the garage wall. The bed lies along that wall too, its head against the north partition, where a framed black rectangle sits. That north partition is an interior wall, with the corner room behind it, so the rectangle is no window. The room keeps its walls in both layouts and gets a door, trim around the panel, its rod refitted and its raw edges cased.
- The sump pit. Open, no lid, no pump, water standing in it, hard against the foundation corner at the rear on the garage side. It needs a pump before anything else goes in, and a sealed lid before the room is finished. Both layouts box it into a closet. The closet's door hangs on the garage wall and swings into the room; nothing that stands beside it is in the way. The pump is on the sellers' list; the closet is the family's.
- The furnace and the water heater. About 12 to 15 feet in from the garage side and north of the landing, the heater nearer the rear, their controls facing that way, the floor drain in the slab south-west of the furnace. Both are original to 2003; the furnace's coil and the condenser outside were replaced in 2023 or 2024, so a replacement one day keeps it. The utility room wraps them with the 30 inches the code wants in front of the controls, runs under the landing's north edge and south into the pocket beside the stair, and its door opens east into the lounge.
- The posts. Two, staggered, under the far beam. The first stands about 23 feet in from the garage side, 14 from the rear, on the hall's east wall line. The second stands about a foot and a half east of it and 4 feet nearer the street, inside the front far room, where it gets boxed. The garage-side beam runs along the corner room's east wall and over the closet in the finished room, boxed in there already; its posts are not marked yet. That first post holds the hall to 3 feet and no wider, and it is why the bath narrowed to 4.3, with the tub turned along the stair wall.
- The doors. The sump closet's opens south into the corner room. The corner room's opens east into the lounge, where the end table used to stand. The utility's opens east into the lounge. The office's opens into the office. The finished room's new door opens into the room. The bath's opens into the bath. In the first layout the studio's opens into the studio; in the second the play area's gate folds back against the front wall.
- The porch bump-out exists. The plan drew no basement under the front projection. The walk-through found it framed off bare concrete, 12 feet wide and about 2 deep, with fiberglass between the joists and a dark band at the base of the wall. It holds the putting tee. The dark band gets a moisture meter at the inspection.
- The panels. One sits on the street wall of the front far room beside its small high window, the other on the finished room's garage wall. Both keep their working space; both are drawn about where the photos put them.
- Insulation. The utility walls have fiberglass in the stud bays. The rest of the perimeter is bare concrete behind the studs and needs insulation before drywall. The sellers framed the perimeter and some partitions but never finished the drywall, so their framing gets checked against the chosen layout before any of it is kept.
- Lighting. One pull-chain bulb lights the rear corner. Every room gets its own lights and outlets at the rough-in.

## How the rooms work

In layout 1, players come in through the slider to the couch and walk the lane down the far side to the studio door, past the office. The lane doubles as the hall, 3 feet wide between the bath and the far beam's first post. Players putt from the tee in the bump-out toward the lounge, 32 feet, and a miss hits the net on the rear wall. The kids are behind a door in the finished room, at the stair's foot, with the counter and the closet holding their things.

In layout 2, the studio door is a few steps left of the slider, and the office's sits down the lane across from the utility room's. Inside the studio the bar is the first thing on the right, the two closets along the rear wall, the table in the middle, the desk down the garage side past it. The play area sits behind the half wall on the far side, in view from the couch.

## Where the furnace is

Seen on November 5 and marked by Nick that evening. The furnace and the gas water heater, an A.O. Smith, stand about 12 to 15 feet off the garage wall and north of the landing. Fiberglass fills the stud walls behind them. Their controls face the garage side, and the floor drain sits in the slab south-west of the furnace. The furnace vents in metal pipe, which says it is a standard-efficiency unit. Both are original to 2003; the coil on the furnace and the condenser outside were replaced in 2023 or 2024.

## What the stair decides

The stair comes down from a door under the loft flight, turns at a landing about five feet off the floor, then lands facing the front wall, with the finished room's doorway immediately to the right of its foot. The treads are open on stringers and framed on both sides; the finished room's closet backs onto the lower run. The landing and the upper run hang in the middle of the room, so the only full-height path to the stair's foot runs down the far side, and the hall takes it as the putting lane.

## The bath

The rough-in sits at the stair's foot, right beside the stringers. A toilet needs 15 inches between its center and anything beside it, 21 clear in front, and the rough-in wants to sit about 12 off the finished wall behind it. Whether the 4.3 by 9 foot bath fits as drawn depends on those three measurements, taken once the basement is cleared. If the center sits too close to the stringers, the bath turns, with the toilet on the hall wall and a short cut in the slab to move the drain: about $100 to $300 in a rented saw and concrete, or $500 to $1,500 hired. The dark staining on the slab around the rough-in was water from the sewer camera inspection, nothing more.

## The studio

- Walls: staggered studs with mineral wool batts in every studio wall, and one layer of drywall to start. Batts run $1.25 to $1.40 for each square foot of wall, about $800 to $1,000 in a room this size. A second layer adds $400 to $600 later if the noise carries. In layout 1 the wall shared with the office matters most.
- Door: solid core, with seals around the frame and a sweep at the bottom, $200 to $400.
- Ceiling: a drop grid with tiles rated to block sound, batts laid on top of them, the microphone and speaker cable above the grid. The four speakers mount in the grid at the corners. About $500 to $900. A 20-amp circuit feeds the audio rack and the game PC.
- Sump closet, layout 2 only: a framed box in the foundation corner around the pit, about 2.5 by 3 feet, with a door plus a sealed lid on the pit, so the pump can be reached and the room stays dry and quiet. About $150 to $250. In layout 1 the same closet stands in the bin room.
- Closet of minis: a framed front 24 inches deep that spans 5 to 6 feet of wall; two pairs of sliding doors close it, with shelves inside. About $350 to $650.
- Inside: acoustic panels at the walls' first reflection points, $35 to $50 each made at home from mineral wool and fabric. The Philips Hue lights go in the corners under the speakers.
- What it adds: over the basement rate of $13 to $17 a square foot that every finished room costs, the sound work is about $1,000 to $1,500 more in materials. Hired out, the same sound work runs about $2,200 to $4,500. Every figure here is an estimate.

## The bar

The bar starts dry: a cabinet with a counter and the mini fridge, with a capped water line left in the wall. A sink comes later if the table wants one. In layout 2, supply is the easy half: the water heater stands a few feet away through the utility wall. The floor drain beside the furnace is close, so if it is on the sewer rather than condensate only, a short line reaches it. In layout 1 the bath's rough-in is across the 3-foot hall, which is a short cut in the slab. Either way most basement bars solve the drain with a small pump under the sink that lifts the water into a line overhead, $350 to $700. The plumbing goes on the basement permit either way.

## Bins

A 27-gallon bin is 31 by 20 inches. A shelf 24 inches deep, with 17 between tiers, takes three bins across every 8 feet. Four tiers stand about six feet tall. A fifth tier reaches a little over seven feet and adds a quarter more bins, if the joists clear it. Layout 2 puts 32 bins in the finished room, where the bed, the desk and the counter stand now, around the panel's working space, with the closet taking the overflow. Layout 1 puts the same 32 in the corner by the utilities, along the rear and garage walls and the one it shares with the finished room, around the sump closet.

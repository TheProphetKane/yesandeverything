# The basement layout for 45 Autumn Crest Ct

The basement is a 36 by 33 foot rectangle outside its 9-inch poured walls, about 34 by 31 inside, plus a 12 by 2 bump-out under the front porch that the walk-through of November 5 found real. This is the layout Nick chose, redrawn that evening around what the walk-through found and what he said about it afterward. Every room stays where he put it. What moved is everything inside the lines. An open sump pit sits hard in the foundation corner, rear, garage side. The furnace and the water heater stand about 9 feet off that wall, their controls facing it, a floor drain before them. Two steel beams run front to back on staggered posts. The sellers already finished a room at the foot of the stair. There are two electrical panels.

Every spot marked "about" comes from the walk-through photos and Nick's description, not a tape. The basement is full of the sellers' belongings, so the layout gets taped onto the slab once it is cleared, and a wall moves if the tape says so.

## What the walk-through changed

- The sump pit. Open, no lid, no pump, water standing in it, hard against the foundation corner, back, garage side, which is the studio's rear corner. The pit needs a pump before anything else goes in, and a sealed lid before the room is finished. The studio boxes it into a closet with a door, so the pit stays reachable. The pump is on the sellers' list; the closet is the family's.
- The furnace. The furnace and the water heater stand about 9 feet off the garage wall, north of the landing, their controls facing that wall, the floor drain before them toward the beam. That takes the studio's front half down to about 8 feet wide, so the table moved there and the desk to the garage wall, and the room keeps its 179 square feet in an L.
- Two beams, not one. Two steel beams run front to back, one each side of the stair about a third of the width in, each on staggered posts. Both land on wall lines in this layout: the garage-side beam in the studio's and the bin room's, the far one in the hall's. That is why the hall is 3 feet wide and no wider, and why the bath narrowed to 4.3 with the tub turned along the stair wall. If the tape puts a post a foot off a wall line, that partition moves to meet it, or the post gets boxed.
- The finished room. The sellers finished the room at the stair's foot on the garage side: teal walls, foam mats over the slab, base cabinets with a counter, a curtain for a door, an electrical panel in a rough cutout, a doorless closet immediately inside on the right with its shelving pulled loose, a framed black rectangle on the far wall, and a jog that narrows the room again where the bed is. The plan had the bin room there, so it keeps its walls and takes the bins, 24 on four tiers against the 44 the old rectangle held. It needs a door, trim around the panel, and its closet shelving refitted. If the rectangle turns out to be a window, this is the one basement room that could ever be a legal bedroom, so that gets settled before the shelves go against that wall. Tearing out the room, or its narrowing wall, stays possible later; nothing in this layout depends on it.
- The porch bump-out exists. The plan drew no basement under the front projection. The walk-through found it framed off bare concrete, 12 feet wide and about 2 deep, with fiberglass along the joists and a dark band along the base of the concrete. It holds the putting tee. The dark band gets a moisture meter at the inspection.
- The panels. One sits on the play area's front wall beside its small high window, the other in the finished room. Both keep their working space; both are drawn about where the photos put them.
- Insulation. The utility walls have fiberglass in the stud bays. The rest of the perimeter is bare concrete behind the studs and needs insulation before drywall. The sellers framed the perimeter and some partitions but never finished the drywall, so their framing gets checked against this layout before any of it is kept.
- Lighting. One pull-chain bulb lights the studio corner. Every room gets its own lights and outlets at the rough-in.

## How the rooms work

Players come in through the slider to the couch, its end stand and the gear table. The studio door is a few steps to the left, and the office's sits down the lane across from the utility room. Inside the studio, the bar is the first thing on the left, the books along the rear wall, the desk down the garage wall past the sump closet, the table in the front half, the minis in the closet at the front.

The lane doubles as the hall, 3 feet wide between the bath and the far beam. Players putt from the tee in the bump-out toward the lounge, 32 feet, and a miss hits the net on the rear wall. The office door is far enough down the wall that a putt does not fly through it, and the play area sits behind the half wall on the far side, in view from the couch.

## Where the furnace is

Seen on November 5. The furnace and the gas water heater, an A.O. Smith, stand about 9 feet off the garage wall, north of the landing, fiberglass filling the stud walls behind them. Their controls face the garage side, and the floor drain sits before them on the slab, toward the beam. Both are original to 2003; the coil on the furnace and the condenser outside were replaced in 2023 or 2024, so a new furnace one day keeps that coil. The utility room wraps them with the 30 by 30 inch working space the code wants in front of the controls, and its door opens to the lounge.

## What the stair decides

The stair comes down from a door under the loft flight, turns at a landing about five feet off the floor, then lands facing the front wall. The treads are open on stringers and framed on both sides, tool storage today. The landing and the upper run hang in the middle of the room, so the only full-height path to the stair's foot runs down the far side, and the hall takes that side as the putting lane. The far beam's wall line is 3 feet from the bath's, which is all the width the hall gets.

## The bath

The rough-in sits at the stair's foot, right beside the stringers. A toilet needs 15 inches between its center and anything beside it, 21 clear in front, and the rough-in wants to sit about 12 off the finished wall behind it. Whether the 4.3 by 9 foot bath fits as drawn depends on those three measurements, taken once the basement is cleared. If the center sits too close to the stringers, the bath turns, with the toilet on the hall wall and a short cut in the slab to move the drain: about $100 to $300 in a rented saw and concrete, or $500 to $1,500 hired. The dark staining on the slab around the rough-in was water from the sewer camera inspection, nothing more.

## The studio

- Walls: staggered studs with mineral wool batts in every studio wall, and one layer of drywall to start. Batts run $1.25 to $1.40 for each square foot of wall, about $800 to $900 in a room this size. A second layer adds $400 to $600 later if the noise carries.
- Door: solid core, with seals around the frame and a sweep at the bottom, $200 to $400.
- Ceiling: a drop grid with tiles rated to block sound, batts laid on top of the tiles, the microphone and speaker cable above the grid. The four speakers mount in the grid at the corners. About $500 to $900. A 20-amp circuit feeds the audio rack and the game PC.
- Sump closet: a framed box around the pit in the rear corner, about 2.5 by 3 feet, with a door, plus a sealed lid on the pit, so the pump stays reachable while the room keeps dry and quiet. About $150 to $250.
- Closet: a framed front across the 6-foot recess at the front on the garage side, 2 feet deep, with two pairs of sliding doors and shelves inside for the minis. About $350 to $650.
- Inside: acoustic panels at the walls' first reflection points, $35 to $50 each made at home from mineral wool and fabric. The Philips Hue lights go in the corners under the speakers.
- What it adds: over the basement rate of $13 to $17 a square foot that every finished room costs, the sound work adds about $1,000 to $1,500 in materials. Hired out, the same sound work runs about $2,200 to $4,500. Every figure here is an estimate.

## The bar

The bar starts dry: a cabinet with a counter and the mini fridge, with a capped water line left in the wall. A sink comes later if the table wants one. Water is the easy half: the water heater stands about 5 feet away, through the utility wall. The drain is the harder half: the floor drain beside the furnace is close, and if it is a real drain on the sewer rather than a condensate drain, a short line reaches it; otherwise the bath rough-in is about 21 feet away past the stair, and most basement bars solve that with a small pump under the sink that lifts the water into a line overhead, $350 to $700. The plumbing goes on the basement permit either way.

## Bins

A 27-gallon bin is 31 by 20 inches. A shelf 24 inches deep, with 17 between tiers, takes three bins across every 8 feet. Four tiers stand about six feet tall. A fifth tier reaches a little over seven feet and adds a quarter more bins, if the joists clear it. In the finished room the shelves run where the bed and the desk stand now, around the panel's working space and the closet: 24 bins on four tiers, 30 on five. The closet takes the overflow once its shelving is refitted, and the old studio corner's shelves are gone with the room.
